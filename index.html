Abstract

A logistic regression and a gradient-boosted ensemble were trained on 90 days of prior search performance to predict which pages will lose visibility over the next 30 days, split by client so no client's content leaks between train and test. Gradient boosting wins on AUC (0.887 vs 0.837), but the simpler logistic regression wins on Precision@50 (0.62 vs 0.52) — the metric that matches how an analyst actually works a fixed-size queue — so it's the model that ships. The result is a five-archetype action playbook, not a black-box score, with every leakage risk tested and every limitation stated plainly below.

01

Problem

  • Unit of analysis: one page (client + content ID), scored on its prior-90-day window.
  • Decision: which pages an analyst opens first in a fixed-size review queue.
  • Cost of a wrong call: a real decline gets missed, or time is spent on a page that didn't need it — recoverable, not safety-critical.
  • Why ML: a fixed rule can weigh one or two conditions at once; page performance depends on several interacting signals (impressions, position, CTR, staleness) that a model can learn jointly instead of via one hand-tuned rule.
  • The FlyRank case: client catalogs run into the hundreds of thousands of pages — most are fine, a minority need attention, and analyst time is the constrained resource. This model triages that, it doesn't replace editorial judgment.
02

Data

FlyRank/internship-warehouse — features from March 2026, label from the separate April 2026 partition, so the outcome window never overlaps the feature window.

  • Excluded: rebuilt product flags (health_score, priority_score) — feeding a rebuilt copy back in as a feature just teaches the model to copy the existing rule.
  • Excluded: next_30d_impressions_delta — built from the label window itself, demonstrated leak (inflates AUC to 1.000).
  • Excluded: hashed IDs used for joins/grouping only, never as features.
  • No client names, URLs, titles, or queries appear anywhere in this release or this paper.
03

Methodology

  • Features (5): log_impressions_90d, avg_position_90d, ctr_90d, days_since_last_update, word_count.
  • Target: future_decline = 1 if next-30-day impressions fall below 70% of prior-90-day impressions.
  • Baseline: a transparent rule — visible (≥250 impressions) and either CTR under 70% of its position tier's expected value, or 180+ days stale.
  • Split: grouped by client (GroupShuffleSplit, 25% held out) — every page from one client lands entirely in train or entirely in test.

05

Limitations

  • Validated on one 90-day window (Mar→Apr 2026) — not yet tested across seasons.
  • Staleness effect is bounded: decline rate rises through 181–365 days, then drops past a year.
  • word_count missing on ~40% of rows, engagement_rate on ~71%.
  • Not causal — a high rank resembles pages that later declined, it doesn't diagnose why.
